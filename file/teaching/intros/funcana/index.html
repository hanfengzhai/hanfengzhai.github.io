Motivation
  • Physics: a heat source scans the part while material conductivity or loading varies with parameter \(\xi\).
  • Goal: predict one probe quantity \(s(\xi)\) accurately instead of storing the full temperature field.
  • Challenge: every new \(\xi\) requires a costly FEM solve \(\Rightarrow\) build a fast reduced surrogate for \(s(\xi)\).

2D steady heat on \(\Omega=(0,1)^2\)
\[ \begin{aligned} -\nabla\!\cdot\!\big(\kappa\,\nabla u\big) &= f \quad \text{in }\Omega, \\ u = 0 \text{ on }\Gamma_D, \qquad &\kappa\,\partial_n u = g \text{ on }\Gamma_N. \end{aligned} \] \[ s = \frac{1}{|\omega|}\int_\omega u\,\mathrm{d}x = Lu . \]
  • \(u\): temperature; \(\kappa(x,\xi)\): parametric conductivity; \(f\): source; \(g\): flux.
  • \(\xi\): scalar scenario parameter (distinct from spatial coordinate \(x\)).
  • \(s\): disk average on \(\omega\) (bounded on \(H^1\); point evaluation is not).
GB Part 7, §5.1 [continuous elliptic]; GB Part 4, Def. 1.68 [bounded \(L\)]
Weak form
\[ \int_\Omega \kappa\,\nabla u\!\cdot\!\nabla v\,\mathrm{d}x = \int_\Omega f\,v\,\mathrm{d}x + \int_{\Gamma_N} g\,v\,\mathrm{d}s \qquad \forall v\in \mathcal{S} . \] \[ a(u,v)=\ell(v). \]
  • \(v\): test function; integration by parts transfers one derivative onto \(v\).
  • \(\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{V}=H^1_{0,\Gamma_D}\): essential BC \(v=0\) on \(\Gamma_D\); natural flux \(g\) enters \(\ell(v)\).
GB Part 7, §5.1 [IBP → bilinear form]; GB Part 6, (3.1) [variational (3.1)]
Well-posedness
\[ \left\Vert u \right\Vert_{L^2}\le C_P\,\left\Vert \nabla u \right\Vert_{L^2}, \qquad a(u,u)\ge \alpha\,\left\Vert u \right\Vert_{H^1}^2 , \] \[ \exists!\,u\in \mathcal{S}:\quad a(u,v)=\ell(v)\quad \forall v\in \mathcal{V} . \]
  • Continuity of \(a\) and \(\ell\) with Poincaré's inequality yields coercivity \(\alpha > 0\).
  • Lax–Milgram guarantees a unique, continuously stable weak solution \(u\in\mathcal{S}\).
GB Part 7, Lem. 5.4 [Poincaré]; GB Part 7, §5.4 [strong coercivity]; GB Part 6, Cor. 3.10 [Lax–Milgram]
Operator form
\[ \langle Au,v \rangle=a(u,v),\qquad \langle b,v \rangle=\ell(v),\qquad Au=b . \]
  • \(\langle \cdot,\cdot \rangle\): duality pairing with \(A:\mathcal{S}\to \mathcal{V}'\) and \(b\in \mathcal{V}'\).
  • Trial \(u\in\mathcal{S}\), test \(v\in\mathcal{V}\); reduced spaces \(\mathcal{S}_h\subset\mathcal{S}\), \(\mathcal{V}_h\subset\mathcal{V}\) with \(u_h\in\mathcal{S}_h\), \(v_h\in\mathcal{V}_h\).
  • Linear output \(s=Lu\) (scalar in the 2D demo; vector \(Z=\mathbb{R}^l\) in Zahm et al.).
GB Part 6, (3.1) [\(Au=b\)]; GB Part 4, Def. 1.68 [bounded \(A\)]; GB Part 4, Def. 1.73 [dual \(\mathcal{V}'\)]; GB Part 3, Def. 1.48 [Hilbert]
Parameter \(\xi\)
\[ A(\xi)\,u(\xi)=b(\xi),\qquad s(\xi)=L(\xi)\,u(\xi),\qquad \xi\in\Xi . \]
  • \(\xi\): physical scenario parameter (conductivity in demo; loads or BCs in general).
  • Many-query workflows (design, UQ, control) evaluate thousands of \(\xi\); full FEM is too costly.
  • Offline–online split: invest offline snapshots at \(\xi_i\) to make every online query \(s(\xi)\) millisecond-fast.
GB Part 6, (3.1) [variational form]; GB Part 4, Def. 1.68 [bounded parametric maps]
Parametric output problem
  • Target: approximate the probe map \(\xi \mapsto s(\xi) = L(\xi)u(\xi)\) directly via reduced spaces \((\mathcal{S}_h, \mathcal{V}_h, W_k^{Q})\).
  • Offline: construct trial, test, and dual bases from snapshots. Online: solve tiny projected systems per \(\xi\).

Adjoints \(A^*\), \(L^*\) and dual operator \(Q\)
\[ \langle Au,v \rangle=\langle u,A^{*}v \rangle \qquad \forall u\in \mathcal{S},\ v\in \mathcal{V} . \] \[ A^{*}Q=L^{*},\qquad Q\in\mathcal{L}(Z',\mathcal{V}),\qquad s=Lu=Q^{*}b . \]
  • \(A^{*}:\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{S}'\) maps test directions to trial functionals; \(L^{*}:Z'\to\mathcal{S}'\) encodes sensor weights.
  • \(Q\in\mathcal{L}(Z',\mathcal{V})\): dual influence operator solving the adjoint PDE for unit output loads.
  • Duality identity \(s = Lu = Q^{*}b\): exact \(u\) or exact \(Q\) alone recovers the output \(s\).
GB Part 4, Thm. 1.82 [Riesz]; GB Part 7, Def. 5.7 [adjoint \(A^*\)]; GB Part 6, Thm. 3.9 [well-posed dual]
What Zahm et al. add
Reduced-Space Architecture
  • Goal-oriented: reduced bases target \(s=Lu\) rather than full-field energy alone.
  • Three spaces: Petrov–Galerkin \(\mathcal{S}_h\neq\mathcal{V}_h\) plus dual space \(W_k^{Q}\).
  • Vector output: \(s\in Z\) via operator dual \(Q\in\mathcal{L}(Z',\mathcal{V})\).
Certification & Adaptivity
  • Certified bounds: computable \(\Delta(\xi)\) from primal and dual residuals.
  • Greedy enrichment: sample snapshots at \(\arg\max_\xi\Delta(\xi)\).
  • Estimator ladder: Petrov–Galerkin (pg) → primal–dual (pd) → saddle-point (sp).
Estimator ladder (pg → pd → sp)

Petrov–Galerkin (pg): \(s_{\mathrm{pg}} = Lu_h\) directly from the projected primal state \(u_h\in\mathcal{S}_h\).

Primal–dual (pd): \(s_{\mathrm{pd}} = Lu_h + Q_h^{*}(b - Au_h)\) corrects \(Lu_h\) using the residual and dual approximation \(Q_h\).

Saddle-point (sp): couples \((\mathcal{S}_h, \mathcal{V}_h, W_k^{Q})\) in a single variational saddle-point system (Prop. 2.10) to achieve quadratic error reduction.

GB Part 6, §3.3 [Ritz–Galerkin]; GB Part 6, §3.4 [discrete PG]; GB Part 6, Thm. 3.9 [BNB]; GB Part 3, Exo. 1.42 [Cauchy–Schwarz]
inf–sup stability
\[ \alpha\left\Vert u \right\Vert_{\mathcal{S}}\le \left\Vert Au \right\Vert_{\mathcal{V}'}\le \beta\left\Vert u \right\Vert_{\mathcal{S}}, \] \[ \alpha=\inf_{u\neq 0}\sup_{v\neq 0}\frac{\langle Au,v \rangle}{\left\Vert u \right\Vert_{\mathcal{S}}\left\Vert v \right\Vert_{\mathcal{V}}},\qquad \beta=\sup_{u\neq 0}\sup_{v\neq 0}\frac{\langle Au,v \rangle}{\left\Vert u \right\Vert_{\mathcal{S}}\left\Vert v \right\Vert_{\mathcal{V}}}. \]
  • \(\alpha>0\) (Banach–Nečas–Babuška): links small residuals directly to small state errors.
  • Handles general \(\mathcal{S}\neq\mathcal{V}\) (Petrov–Galerkin); symmetric heat (\(\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{V}\)) recovers Lax–Milgram.
GB Part 6, Thm. 3.9 [BNB]; GB Part 6, Cor. 3.10 [Lax–Milgram]
Petrov–Galerkin projection
\[ \langle Au_h-b,\,v_h \rangle=0\qquad \forall v_h\in\mathcal{V}_h,\qquad u_h\in\mathcal{S}_h\subset \mathcal{S} . \] \[ \left\Vert u-u_h \right\Vert_{\mathcal{S}} \le \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\delta_{\mathcal{S}_h,\mathcal{V}_h}^2}}\;\min_{w_h\in\mathcal{S}_h}\left\Vert u-w_h \right\Vert_{\mathcal{S}} . \]
  • Residual \(Au_h-b\) is orthogonal to test space \(\mathcal{V}_h\) (\(\mathcal{S}_h\neq\mathcal{V}_h\) in general).
  • Quasi-optimality holds whenever test-space defect \(\delta_{\mathcal{S}_h,\mathcal{V}_h}<1\) (Prop. 2.1).
Test-space defect \(\delta_{\mathcal{S}_h,\mathcal{V}_h}\) and discrete inf–sup \(m_h\)

\(\delta_{\mathcal{S}_h,\mathcal{V}_h}\) measures the sine of the angle between \(A\mathcal{S}_h\) and the Riesz lift of \(\mathcal{V}_h\); keeping \(\delta_{\mathcal{S}_h,\mathcal{V}_h} < 1\) is equivalent to a strictly positive discrete inf–sup constant \(m_h > 0\).

GB Part 6, §3.4 [discrete PG, eq. (3.9)]; GB Part 6, (3.10) [discrete inf–sup \(m_h\)]; GB Part 6, Lem. 3.11 [Céa]; GB Part 6, §3.3 [Ritz–Galerkin]
Output error bound (Prop. 2.1, Eq. 9)
\[ \left\Vert s-Lu_h \right\Vert_Z\le \delta^L_{\mathcal{V}_h}\cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\delta_{\mathcal{S}_h,\mathcal{V}_h}^2}}\cdot \min_{w_h\in\mathcal{S}_h}\left\Vert u-w_h \right\Vert_{\mathcal{S}} . \]
  • Output error scales with the product of dual test error \(\delta^L_{\mathcal{V}_h}\) and trial best-approximation error.
  • Compliant case (\(\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{V}\), \(\mathcal{V}_h=\mathcal{S}_h\), \(Lu=\langle b,u \rangle\)): quadratic accuracy \(|s-Lu_h|\le\left\Vert u-u_h \right\Vert_{\mathcal{S}}^2\).
GB Part 6, Lem. 3.11 [Céa quasi-opt.]; GB Part 3, Exo. 1.42 [Cauchy–Schwarz]; GB Part 7, §5.2 [Ritz–Galerkin]
Two jobs of \(\mathcal{V}_h\)
\[ \left\Vert s-Lu_h \right\Vert_Z\le \underbrace{\delta^L_{\mathcal{V}_h}}_{\text{(c) dual range}} \;\cdot\; \underbrace{\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-\delta_{\mathcal{S}_h,\mathcal{V}_h}^2}}}_{\text{(b) PG stability}} \;\cdot\; \underbrace{\min_{w_h\in\mathcal{S}_h}\left\Vert u-w_h \right\Vert_{\mathcal{S}}}_{\text{(a) trial approx.}} . \]
  • \(\mathcal{V}_h\) must simultaneously stabilize projection on \(\mathcal{S}_h\) and capture \(\mathrm{range}(A^{-*}L^*) = \mathrm{range}(Q)\).
  • The 2D heat demo uses a shared snapshot space; Zahm et al. decouple \(\mathcal{V}_h\neq\mathcal{S}_h\) for non-symmetric problems.
GB Part 6, §3.4 [discrete test spaces]; GB Part 6, Thm. 3.9 [inf–sup stability]
Dual problem
\[ A^{*}Q=L^{*},\qquad s=Lu=Q^{*}Au=Q^{*}b . \]
  • Physical meaning: for the probe average on \(\omega\), \(Q\) is the thermal response to a unit source on \(\omega\).
  • Goal sensitivity: \(Q\) identifies exactly which spatial modes of the residual \(b-Au_h\) perturb \(s\).
  • Reduced approximation: building a dedicated dual space \(W_k^{Q}\subset\mathcal{V}\) decouples output accuracy from \(\mathcal{V}_h\).
GB Part 4, Thm. 1.82 [Riesz]; GB Part 7, Def. 5.7 [adjoint \(A^*\)]; GB Part 6, Thm. 3.9 [well-posedness]
Primal–dual estimate
\[ \tilde s = Lu_h + Q_h^{*}\,(b-Au_h), \] \[ \left\Vert s-\tilde s \right\Vert_Z\le \left\Vert u-u_h \right\Vert_{\mathcal{S}}\;\left\Vert L^{*}-A^{*}Q_h \right\Vert_{Z'\to \mathcal{S}'} . \]
  • \(\tilde s\) augments the primal prediction \(Lu_h\) with a dual-weighted residual correction \(Q_h^{*}(b-Au_h)\).
  • Uses three reduced spaces: trial \(\mathcal{S}_h\), test \(\mathcal{V}_h\), and dual \(W_k^{Q}\).
Saddle-point estimator remark (Prop. 2.10)

Zahm et al. (Prop. 2.10) also formulate a saddle-point estimator \(s_{\mathrm{sp}}\) solving jointly over \((\mathcal{S}_h, W_k^{Q})\), avoiding separate test-space coupling while retaining product-of-errors decay.

GB Part 3, Exo. 1.42 [C–S on pairing]; GB Part 4, Thm. 1.82 [Riesz]; GB Part 7, Def. 5.7 [adjoint]; GB Part 3, Def. 1.51 [orthogonal projection]
Certified bound
\[ \left\Vert s(\xi)-\tilde s(\xi) \right\Vert_Z \le \frac{\left\Vert A(\xi)u_h-b(\xi) \right\Vert_{\mathcal{S}_0'}\;\left\Vert L^{*}(\xi)-A^{*}(\xi)Q_h(\xi) \right\Vert_{Z'\to \mathcal{S}_0'}}{\alpha(\xi)} =:\Delta(\xi). \]
  • \(\Delta(\xi)\) is computable online from primal residual \(\times\) dual residual divided by lower bound \(\alpha(\xi)\).
  • Certification: true error never exceeds \(\Delta(\xi)\), so effectivity \(\Delta(\xi)/\left\Vert s-\tilde s \right\Vert_Z \ge 1\).
GB Part 6, (3.5) [inf–sup const. \(m\)]; GB Part 6, Thm. 3.9 [BNB stability]; GB Part 3, Exo. 1.42 [residual product]
Greedy enrichment
\[ \xi^\star \in \arg\max_{\xi\in\Xi}\Delta(\xi), \] \[ \mathcal{S}_h \leftarrow \mathcal{S}_h + \mathrm{span}\,u(\xi^\star), \qquad W_k^{Q} \leftarrow W_k^{Q} + \mathrm{range}\,Q(\xi^\star) . \]
  • Select worst parameter \(\xi^\star\) via \(\Delta(\xi)\), compute full primal \(u(\xi^\star)\) and dual \(Q(\xi^\star)\), and enrich bases.
  • Iterate until \(\max_{\xi\in\Xi}\Delta(\xi)\) falls below the target tolerance.

Takeaways
  1. State field \(u\) is intermediate; vector output \(s=Lu\) with certified bound \(\Delta(\xi)\) is the objective.
  2. Weak formulation \(Au=b\) and inf–sup stability \(\alpha > 0\) yield rigorous a posteriori bounds.
  3. Primal–dual correction multiplies primal and dual errors, driving relative QoI error to \(\sim 10^{-13}\).
  4. On 80 queries of 2D steady heat, online PROM delivers \(\approx 222\times\) speedup over full FEM.
